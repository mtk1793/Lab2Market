## CRITICAL INSIGHTS FOR ADDMANUCHAIN

### 1. What to Protect and How

| AddManuChain Asset | Best Protection |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Platform software code | Copyright (automatic) + Patent (if novel method) |
| AI algorithm / recommendation logic | Trade Secret OR Patent (depends on reverse-engineering risk) |
| Platform name / logo / brand | Trademark — file in Canada first, then PCT/Madrid |
| DRM token workflow (novel process) | Patent — this is a core innovation worth filing |
| Digital blueprint verification process | Patent or Trade Secret |
| Physical appearance of any hardware product | Industrial Design |

---

### 2. The AI Disclosure Warning — Directly Relevant to Our Work

**Risk:** We use AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, etc.) to help draft strategy, product specs, and pitch decks. If we input un-filed invention details into an open AI platform, we may have:
- Triggered a **public disclosure event** (killing Canadian and foreign patent rights)
- Lost **trade secret protection** for that information

**Action required:**
- Use closed/private AI environments for sensitive IP (or ensure enterprise privacy settings)
- Do NOT describe core invention mechanics to open AI tools until after provisional patent filing
- Vet all AI-generated content with an IP lawyer before using it in any formal document

---

### 3. The "No AI as Inventor" Rule

AI cannot be listed as an inventor in any jurisdiction. If AI substantially generated an idea, the patentability of that idea is at risk. This means:
- All inventive contributions must come from human inventors (you, co-founders, advisors)
- AI should be used as a tool, not as a co-inventor

**For AddManuChain:** Our AI assistant that generates insights or recommendations is a feature of the product — not an inventor. Distinguish clearly between the platform (protectable) and what the platform does with AI (a function of the product).

---

### 4. Trade Secret vs. Patent Decision

For our DRM token pipeline and AI matching logic, we need to make an explicit decision:

| Factor | Lean Patent | Lean Trade Secret |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Can competitors reverse-engineer it? | Yes → Patent | No → Trade Secret |
| Is the method publicly visible in use? | Yes → Patent | No → Trade Secret |
| Do you need to license it? | Yes → Patent | No → Trade Secret |
| Do you want 20-year exclusivity in law? | Yes → Patent | — |

**Recommendation from session:** Talk to an IP lawyer. Don't default to patents — for processes that are truly hard to discover (like our AI training approach or specific weighting algorithms), trade secrets may be more valuable long-term.

---

### 5. Priority Filing Order

Based on the session guidance, for a startup at our stage:

1. **Provisional patent** (placeholder — buys 12 months, low cost, stakes the date)
2. **Trademark** (name and logo — file in Canada, then Madrid for international)
3. **PCT filing** within 12 months of provisional (if going international with patents)
4. **Copyright** (automatic — just keep dated records of all code commits and design files)
5. **Industrial Design** (if we ever build a physical hardware product)

---

### 6. International Strategy

- We plan to eventually be in the US, EU, and Middle East (offshore O&G markets)
- This means: **PCT for patents**, **Madrid for trademarks** — file Canada first, then international
- More than 3 jurisdictions = Madrid/PCT is almost always cheaper than filing individually
