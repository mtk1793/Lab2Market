## SECTION 1 — His Role & SLB's AM Operation

**Q1.** You're responsible for expanding AM tools at SRPC — can you walk me through what that actually looks like day to day? What types of parts are you printing and for which applications?

**Q2.** You've overseen 11,000+ LPBF parts delivered — that's a significant scale. What does the qualification and approval process look like for a new part at SLB? Who signs off, and what standards are you working to?

**Q3.** Where does your team sit in SLB's supply chain structure — are you a production facility for internal SLB tools and downhole equipment, or are you also serving external clients?

**Q4.** You have deep experience with both LPBF and LMD (your École Polytechnique research). When does SLB choose one process over the other? What are the practical trade-offs in your context?

---

## SECTION 2 — Materials & Qualification Pain Points

**Q5.** From a materials perspective, what are the biggest bottlenecks you face when qualifying a new alloy or a new part geometry for AM? Is it the data, the certification bodies, the internal approval process — or something else?

**Q6.** One concept I'm building is a shared material properties database — tested mechanical data (yield, tensile, fatigue, elongation) for AM alloys in offshore-relevant conditions, validated by class societies like DNV GL or Lloyd's. Does a gap like that actually exist in your world, or does SLB generate and own all that data internally?

**Q7.** When you're dealing with exotic materials — Inconel, titanium, duplex stainless — how confident are you that the mechanical property data you're using for qualification is representative of *your* process specifically, vs. generic published data? Is that a real concern?

**Q8.** What's SLB's approach to post-processing (HIP, heat treatment) for LPBF parts? Is that standardized, or still case-by-case?

---

## SECTION 3 — Digital File & IP Governance

**Q9.** When SLB prints a part — especially one designed or co-designed with an OEM or a client — how is the digital file managed? Is there a formal DRM or access control process, or is it mostly handled through internal IT systems?

**Q10.** Have you encountered situations where an OEM was reluctant to share a CAD file or blueprint because of IP concerns — and how did you work around that?

**Q11.** If SLB wanted to print a part at an *external* certified facility — say in a remote location where your own SRPC can't reach — is there a process for that today, or does it not happen?

---

## SECTION 4 — External AM Supply Chain & Platform Relevance

**Q12.** SLB operates on every continent. When a field operation needs an AM part urgently and your Paris facility can't deliver in time — what happens today? Is there a network of certified external printers SLB can call on, or is it still "wait and ship"?

**Q13.** I'm building a platform that connects industrial operators to certified AM facilities — with a DRM layer for IP protection and a certification matching engine. From SLB's perspective as both a major operator *and* an AM producer, does that solve a real problem, or would SLB just develop that capability internally?

**Q14.** Do you see a market for a shared, industry-neutral material properties library for AM in oil & gas — something SLB could both contribute to *and* draw from? Or is that too sensitive competitively?

---

## SECTION 5 — AM Adoption Barriers & Industry Trajectory

**Q15.** You reposted the Framatome nuclear AM centre announcement — that's a significant investment in AM for nuclear and defence. Do you see oil & gas moving toward that level of AM commitment, or is it still slower adoption?

**Q16.** In your experience, what is the single biggest reason AM adoption is still slower than the technology deserves in O&G? Is it certification, cost, lack of qualified printers, conservative engineering culture — or something else entirely?

**Q17.** Regulation and certification come up in almost every interview I do as the #1 barrier. From your position at SLB — is the answer to work *within* existing standards (DNV GL, API, ASME), or does the industry need new AM-specific standards developed from scratch?

**Q18.** What does the next 3–5 years look like for AM at SLB and in oilfield services broadly? Where do you think the technology goes?
