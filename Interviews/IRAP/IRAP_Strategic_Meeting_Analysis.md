## KEY MEETING INSIGHTS

### THEME 1: The Liability Question is Your #1 Blocker

**The Core Problem IRAP Raised:**

When a 3D-printed part fails in operational use, **who is liable?**
- The OEM (who owns the design)?
- The printing facility (operator error)?
- The material supplier (defective powder)?
- AddManuChain (platform operator)?
- The part user (mishandling)?

**Your Current Response:** *"We're still working on this. People have different ideas. We'll figure out root cause and assign liability based on origin of failure."*

**IRAP's Assessment:** This is **not acceptable for market entry**, especially in defense/aerospace where liability is literally a life-or-death question.

**Why This Matters:**
- Defense/aerospace procurement teams will NOT sign contracts with undefined liability
- Insurance companies will NOT insure your platform if liability chain is unclear
- Operators will demand indemnification or won't use your service
- This is a **business model blocker**, not just a legal detail

**What IRAP Wants to See:**
A clear framework showing:
1. **Pre-certification requirements** for all parties (machines, operators, materials)
2. **Quality assurance layers** (destructive testing, inspection protocols)
3. **Liability assignment mechanism** (who verifies compliance, who bears risk)
4. **Insurance/warranty structure** (what do you cover, what does OEM cover, what does user accept)

**Implication:** Before pursuing IRAP funding, resolve liability model. This is foundational.

### THEME 2: Certification Complexity is Deeper Than You're Describing

**The Specific Challenge IRAP Flagged:**

You said: *"Certification authorities certify the process, material, people, and machines. Testing labs do non-destructive and destructive testing."*

IRAP response: *"Who certifies at the point of printing on an operational platform? How do we ensure quality when parts are printed in remote locations?"*

**The Real Problem:**
- **Lab setting (controlled):** Easy to certify—everything pre-validated, processes standardized
- **Operational setting (distributed):** Much harder—variations in operator skill, environmental conditions, operator drift over time

**Your Answer (via co-founder):** 
*"We focus on pre-certified facilities. Atlantic Excel got DNV certification for printing. We batch-test samples (100-200 units) before delivery to end-users. We guarantee 98-99% quality."*

**IRAP's Concern:** 
- 98% is not sufficient for mission-critical parts (aerospace, defense demand 99.97%+)
- Batch testing is **statistical assurance**, not individual part guarantee
- What happens when the 2% defect appears in the field?

**What This Means:**
- Your platform must have **real-time quality monitoring** (in-machine sensing, post-print inspection automation)
- Batch testing alone is insufficient; need per-print verification
- This is Phase 2-3 work (you're not there yet)
- Liability depends on this—if you can't guarantee per-print quality, you can't guarantee liability assignment

**Implication:** Advance quality control tech (inline inspection, AI-powered defect detection) is a DIFFERENTIATOR and LIABILITY NECESSITY.

### THEME 3: Your Business Model is Still Undefinable

**The Question IRAP Asked:**

*"Who pays? The part user or the OEM?"*

**Your Response:**
*"It's complex. Primarily OEM pays subscription. OEM gets per-print commission when parts are sold. Part users also pay per-print. Revenue model is subscription + commissions."*

**The Problem IRAP Identified:**

This creates conflicting incentives:
- **If OEM pays subscription:** Why would they share IP? How do they make ROI if they're also charged per-print?
- **If part user pays:** Why would OEM participate? They're not making money directly.
- **If both pay:** You're double-dipping, which erodes value prop for both.

**Your Explanation (Co-founder):**
*"OEM makes money by licensing digital designs to end-users (micro-transactions). They don't make money on machine sales anymore, they make money on IP licensing. This creates a new revenue stream they don't have today."*

**IRAP's Real Concern:**

This is a **fundamental business model disruption**. You're asking OEMs to:
1. Abandon aftermarket spare parts revenue (40-60% margins historically)
2. Embrace digital licensing and per-print royalties (uncertain, unpredictable margins)
3. Trust your platform with their most valuable IP (encryption, token-based access)

**The Viability Question:**
Will OEMs actually do this? Or will they see it as a threat to their business model and block participation?

**The Evidence You Provided:**
- You've talked to 60+ companies and industries
- Aerospace/defense companies (Galaxy, Reaction Dynamics) showed interest
- You haven't closed any contracts yet
- Revenue model is "subject to change" as you learn

**IRAP's Take:** You're building a solution that disrupts OEM business models before OEMs have agreed to participate. This is high-risk.

**Implication:** Before pursuing IRAP funding, secure at least 1-2 anchor OEM partners who explicitly commit to digital licensing model.

### THEME 6: Government Procurement (Defense) is Pathway, But Different Rules Apply

**What IRAP Emphasized:**

*"If you're targeting defense procurement, you need someone responsible for procurement... because there are different rules for defense than other industries."*

**Defense Procurement Realities (Implied):**
- **Compliance Requirements:** Weapons systems certification is EXTREME
- **IP Sensitivity:** Government wants to ensure supply chain security
- **Vendor Management:** Pre-qualified vendors, dual-sourcing requirements
- **Timeline:** 12-24 months from pilot to contract
- **Price:** Not as price-sensitive as commercial, but must meet performance/security standards
- **Relationships:** Vendor relationships are CRITICAL; it's not transactional

**Your Strategic Shift:**
You moved from Oil & Gas (slow, hard to certify) to Aerospace/Defense (faster, more innovative).

**IRAP's View:**
Smart pivot, BUT you need someone with **defense procurement experience** on the team. Otherwise you'll waste cycles on approach.

**Implication:** Hire or advise someone with DND (Canadian Department of National Defense) or DARPA (equivalent) experience BEFORE pursuing defense customers.

### THEME 7: IRAP Funding is Project-Based, Not Operational

**What IRAP Clearly Stated:**

*"We pay labor costs for innovation work, not operational costs. We're advisory foremost, but have some project-based funding. We don't typically pay for regular operational or development work. It's innovation in the back with interest us."*

**Translation:**
- ✅ IRAP can fund: "Develop real-time quality monitoring system for distributed printing" (innovation)
- ✅ IRAP can fund: "Design liability framework for multi-party AM ecosystem" (strategic innovation)
- ✅ IRAP can fund: "Pilot certification pathway with DNV for defense applications" (innovation de-risking)
- ❌ IRAP can't fund: "Build user interface for order management" (operational software development)
- ❌ IRAP can't fund: "Hire general software engineers" (operational headcount)
- ❌ IRAP can't fund: "Pay salaries for running business" (operational costs)

**Cost Sharing Structure:**
*"We only pay for labour. Non-labor costs (supplies, materials, equipment) you must fund. We pay a portion of labor, and you must show ability to contribute to complement what we provide."*

**Translation:**
- IRAP covers maybe 40-60% of labor for innovation projects
- You cover 40-60% of labor + 100% of non-labor costs
- You need co-funding (venture capital, grants, founder equity)

**Implication:** IRAP is a COMPLEMENT to venture funding, not a substitute. You still need to raise capital.
