# IRAP and SMART: Key Insights & Strategic Analysis

**Metadata**: Date Context: 2026 | NRC-IRAP and EURICA-SMART | Topic: International Research Collaboration and Funding Programs for Advanced Manufacturing

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The transcript reveals two key international collaboration programs: NRC-IRAP (National Research Council Industrial Research Assistance Program) which supports Canadian SMEs in international projects, and EURICA-SMART, a European international network focused on advanced manufacturing collaboration. Together, they provide pathways for Canadian manufacturers to engage in international R&D projects with funding support, particularly through the SMART cluster which focuses on advancing competitiveness in advanced manufacturing through international cooperation.

## Technical Insights
- EURICA-SMART is part of a 47-country international network focused on manufacturing innovation
- SMART cluster specifically targets advancement of factory sector and competitiveness in advanced manufacturing
- Projects typically involve 3-5 countries with durations of 2-3 years
- Average project budget is €1.5-2 million over 3 years, with NRC-IRAP funding up to 50% of Canadian participant costs (max ~$400-500K CAD)
- Focus on market-oriented R&D with full value chain involvement in project proposals
- Technology review processes define application areas and decline certain technological domains periodically

## Market Positioning
- Positions as facilitator of international industrial collaboration and technology transfer
- Bridges global purpose with private execution through established networks
- Leverages existing industrial base, customer relationships, and capital momentum
- Focuses on de-risking international collaboration through established frameworks and partner validation
- Targets mature SMEs with revenue (~$500K/year minimum) and proven technology ready for international projects

## Strategic Implications for AddManuChain
- Demonstrates successful model of government-supported international R&D collaboration
- Shows value of cluster-based approaches for sector-specific international cooperation
- Highlights importance of pre-validation and partner vetting processes
- Provides template for combining domestic funding (IRAP) with international networks (EURICA)
- Illustrates how manufacturing innovation can benefit from global value chain integration

## Challenges Identified
- Requires companies to have sufficient revenue and track record (not for early startups)
- Complex application process requiring international partner coordination
- Need for national funding organization approval from all participating countries
- Projects require significant time commitment (2-3 years) and resource allocation
- Balance needed between civilian focus and potential future defense applications
- Administrative overhead for international project management and reporting

## Recommendations
1. Position AddManuChain as a vehicle for Canadian manufacturers to engage in EURICA-SMART projects
2. Develop partnership models with NRC-IRAP to support Canadian SME participation in international manufacturing collaborations
3. Create pre-qualification programs to help manufacturers meet EURICA-SMART eligibility requirements
4. Establish Canadian partner finding and vetting services for international manufacturing projects
5. Develop project management support services for international R&D collaborations
6. Create awareness programs about EURICA-SMART opportunities within Canadian manufacturing networks
