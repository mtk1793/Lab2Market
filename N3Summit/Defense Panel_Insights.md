# Defense Panel: Industrial Strategy & Investment Landscape — Key Insights

**Metadata**: Date Context: 2026 | Event: N3 Summit Defense Panel | Participants: BDC Capital, Strong North Defense Fund, defense SMEs, policy experts

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Canada's defense industrial strategy represents a fundamental shift from decades of dependency to domestic capability building. The panel revealed a critical inflection point: after 50 years of policy disincentives and fragmented investment, defense technology has transformed from "uninvestable" to national priority within an 8-month window. However, significant gaps persist between strategic ambition and SME reality—particularly in scaling capacity, protecting IP, and reforming procurement timelines that stretch 16+ years from requirement to delivery. The funding ecosystem is evolving rapidly, with $6.3B in committed capital flowing through BDC's platform and the Strong North $300M fund, yet the manufacturing base lacks the industrial capacity to meet 70% Canadian content requirements, creating both opportunity and urgency for new entrants like AddManuChain.

## FUNDING ECOSYSTEM MAPPING

- **Strong North $300M**: Mandated as first-mover lead investor catalyzing partnerships; operates within BDC's $6B defense platform; focused on net-new capability gaps rather than existing industrial base; requires specific DIS alignment and co-investment relationships with other funds

- **BDC $6B Defense Platform**: Comprehensive deployment mechanism across financial advisory, growth capital, and transformational capital; all business lines can access components; local BDC advisors key to identifying defense applicability and providing capital transition support

- **Co-investment Requirements**: Defense investments structured as partnerships between Strong North, ngén, and Circuit Capital; defense-focused funds cannot close rounds without collaboration; capital stacks designed to de-risk toxic balance sheets while maintaining patient investment horizons

- **Patient Capital vs. Traditional VC**: Defense investments demand 7-10 year horizons versus typical 5-7 year VC timelines; capital must be sustained through procurement cycles; coexistence of multiple master priorities: defense signal validity AND financial fiduciary responsibility

- **Geographic Distribution**: National coverage through BDC advisor network; Strong North positioned as lead investor coordinating regional partnerships; focus on Atlantic (shipbuilding), Quebec (aerospace), Ontario (AI), Alberta (energy integration) capabilities

- **Funding Gaps - Middle Tier Cliff**: Policy structure incentivizes SMEs to remain small; transition from $1M to $100M+ revenue creates tax and compliance cliffs; CCPC tax structures, SR&ED claims, and ITB requirements designed for small enterprises, not medium-sized scaling operations

- **IRAP/SDTC Integration**: Traditional innovation funding channels being rewired for defense applications; companies pursuing dual-use strategies to access both commercial and defense capital stacks simultaneously

## INDUSTRIAL STRATEGY PRIORITIES

- **Arctic Sovereignty Infrastructure**: 75% of Arctic coastline requiring deep-water ports, heavy icebreakers, and northern roads to enable personnel/equipment movement; defense industrial strategy emphasis on "build before partner and buy" creates opportunity for domestic manufacturing capacity in harsh environment systems

- **Quantum and AI Sovereign Capabilities**: Canada maintains definitive comparative advantage in quantum research and IP (Waterloo/Kitchener corridor); AI leadership position squandered through IP outflow to US; defense strategy provides "mulligan" to commercialize and retain strategic technologies domestically

- **Advanced Materials Manufacturing**: Additive manufacturing, composites, and forward-deployable production systems critical for distributed manufacturing; sea-container self-contained factories emerging as 40-foot deployable manufacturing capability for Arctic/military operations

- **Maritime Defense Integration**: Shipbuilding capabilities in Atlantic provinces require domestic supply chains; coastal defense systems need EO/IR (electro-optical/infrared) systems where Canada maintains leading position; UAV counter-drone technologies proven effective in Ukraine applications

- **Dual-Use Technology Enablers**: Business model shift toward "as-a-service" delivery (manufacturing-as-a-service, ISR-as-a-service, sensor fusion-as-a-service); private sector venture-backed companies moving faster than traditional government procurement cycles

- **Sovereign Capability Domains**: Ten critical areas where Canada must maintain domestic production to pose credible threat/deterrence; includes energy infrastructure (small modular reactors), EO/IR systems, and Arctic domain awareness capabilities

- **Tradable Mass Concept**: Shift from exquisite multi-billion dollar platforms to cost-effective attributable systems; need for 300 unmanned submarines vs. single $24B manned platform; manufacturing speed and agility over traditional platform dominance

## CHALLENGES & GAPS

- **Procurement Timeline Disconnect**: 16+ year cycle from requirement identification to delivery; government procurement reform urgent but incomplete; accountability layers designed to prevent spending create self-imposed constraints that shrink delivery capacity

- **SME Growth Cliff**: Policy incentives structured to keep companies small; CPC structures, SR&ED, and ITB policies penalize growth beyond small business threshold; "handful of large businesses, very few in the middle" creates industrial capacity bottleneck

- **IP Leakage Risks**: Canadian IP flowing to US/China due to weak contract structures; "government subsidized IP becomes free IP for United States"; terrible intellectual property contracts written by Quebec lawyers enable value extraction without proper return to Canadian economy

- **Supply Chain Fragility**: Single-source dependencies for critical components; 1400-component ventilator example showed 80%+ Chinese sourcing during COVID emergency; lack of domestic advanced manufacturing capability forces offshore reliance

- **Skills Shortages**: Advanced manufacturing workforce gaps in critical technologies; decades of capability loss require rebuilding from foundational levels; "the technology is not here, the capabilities, not here" - skill sets missing for defense-grade manufacturing

- **Regulatory Burden Overlap**: Multiple funding programs with conflicting requirements; companies must navigate overlapping IRAP, SDTC, provincial, and federal programs; compliance overhead consumes resources needed for actual capability development

- **Export Control Complexities**: Dual-use technologies face restrictive controls that limit market access; regulatory uncertainty creates investment risk barriers; companies must maintain compliance while pursuing global opportunities

- **Valley of Death Amplification**: R&D to first procurement gap amplified 1000x in defense sector; companies operating critical infrastructure components with broken balance sheets; toxic capital structures prevent sustainable growth without intervention
