### WESCO Distribution Overview & Context

- **Insight:** If approaching WESCO, you need to go through the right function, not just operations

### WESCO's Supplier Selection & Qualification Model (CS.1 VALIDATED)

**The Core Model: Distributor, Not Manufacturer**
- WESCO does NOT develop products for specialized customers
- WESCO ONLY distributes products supplied by others
- They carry products from approved suppliers only

**Supplier Selection Criteria (Packed with insights):**
1. **Market Presence & Reputation:** Is the supplier known in the market? What's their standing?
2. **Global Market Share:** Supplier's competitive position nationally/globally
3. **Product Reputation:** Brand recognition and quality perception
4. **Quality Practices & Capability:** Do they meet WESCO's standards?
5. **Ability to Serve Without Interruption:** Supply chain reliability is critical
6. **Exclusivity Model:** WESCO typically has **ONE major supplier per product category**
   - Example: "If we're selling a set of forward [products], it will only be Apple... it will only be Samsung"
   - This is NOT negotiable; product exclusivity drives the model

**Backup Suppliers:** Only used if primary supplier has supply disruptions
- Mostly one-supplier relationship per category
- Rare backup arrangements when service risk is high

### The Custom & Specialized Component Challenge (CS.2 VALIDATED)
**Key Finding: Enterprise distributors don't solve custom component problems**

When customers request specialized/custom components WESCO doesn't carry:
- **WESCO's response: "We do not develop any products for any specialized customer"**
- **They are NOT a product development partner**
- **They only stock commodity/high-volume items** in warehouse
  - Example: "If we ever had a million SKUs in use, we might only have 20 on shelf"

**Workaround for Special Orders:**
1. Customer requests specific quantity of specialty item
2. WESCO places order with supplier (special order, made-to-order)
3. Either:
   - Ships directly supplier → customer (common for specials)
   - Or comes through WESCO if bundled with other shipments
4. **Timeline:** Long lead time (mentioned "not a due to deploy in the week. Time which longs around it" - unclear exact duration, but clearly significant)

**Implication:** This is NOT your distribution channel for custom 3D-printed parts. WESCO's model requires volume, reputation, and existing supplier relationships.

### Supply Chain Resilience & Tariff Impact Post-COVID

**Tariff Impact (Lower than Expected):**
- Different from what the news suggests
- Only ~1-4 products directly affected by tariffs
- **Did NOT significantly impact WESCO's business** because they could pass costs to customers
- This is a **pass-through cost model** - WESCO doesn't absorb supplier cost increases

**Post-COVID Local Sourcing Pressure:**
- Mentioned (Aurangzeb acknowledged concern about relying on China/Europe)
- But in practice, tariffs haven't forced major localization
- Supply chain resilience hasn't driven demand for new local suppliers (contrary to some assumptions)

### Procurement Decision Factors (VP.5 - Certification & Trust)

**What Moves the Needle on New Suppliers:**
The key question: "If you have a supplier offering new technology, what factors determine if WESCO works with them?"

**Aurangzeb's Answer:**
1. **Reputation in the Market** (primary)
2. **Supply Chain Presence & Strain** (operational stability)
3. **Acceptability of the Product** (market validation)

**What DOESN'T appear to move the needle:**

- They wait for existing suppliers to invest in new tech

### What This Tells Us About Enterprise Distribution Sales

**WESCO's position in supply chain:**
- Gateway between suppliers and end customers (utilities, contractors, industrial manufacturers, broadband operators)
- They ARE the "certified vendor list" problem solver for their customers
- But they themselves face the same supplier selection problem you're trying to solve
- They've optimized for supplier relationships, not for finding new suppliers

**The Gap Lab2Market Could Fill (but WESCO may not be the buyer):**
- Enterprise distributors like WESCO have EXISTING supplier relationships
- They're not actively shopping for new suppliers unless a customer forces the issue
- For special/custom components: customer + supplier = direct relationship (WESCO just manages logistics)

**Implication:** WESCO is a CHANNEL to end customers, not a buyer of your platform
